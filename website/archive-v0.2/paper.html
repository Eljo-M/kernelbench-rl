KernelBench-RL: Verification, Execution Feedback, and Reproducible GPU Kernel Synthesis

Eljo Mustafaj

Technical report · not presented as a peer-reviewed publication

Download searchable PDFCode and experimental artifacts

Abstract

KernelBench-RL is an execution-graded GPU kernel synthesis system for a memory-constrained workstation. It combines a custom thirty-operator registry, curated Triton implementations, numerical checks, bounded execution, supervised initialization and group-relative optimization. The suite is distinct from the complete official KernelBench benchmark. Fixed baselines passed 180/180 operator/precision/input-seed checks, with geometric mean eager speedups of 2.494x in FP32 and 5.417x in BF16. Verified compiled comparisons cover twenty-nine operators. These are fixed-implementation measurements, not learned-policy gains. Corrected v2 experiments use training-only supervision, correctness-gated speed rewards, separate fresh and inspected development cohorts, and an SFT-only control. For each fresh N=3 cohort, methods are original-base pass@1, base best-of-eight, SFT-only pass@1 and hybrid pass@1. V2 0.49B FP32 recorded respective passes 0/3, 0/3, 0/3 and 0/3, while v2 7B rank 8 recorded 0/3, 0/3, 2/3 and 2/3. A separate prospective v3 study starts from the original pinned 7B base with fresh rank-16 adapters, trains on the former v2 test operators, and uses a different three-operator fresh cohort. Its respective passes were 0/3, 0/3, 2/3 and 2/3. Results remain separated by model, precision, configuration and protocol. The hybrid includes SFT; update counts alone do not establish useful learning, and no RL-only causal gain is inferred. The study preserves failures, checkpoint identities, timing boundaries and unavailable comparisons. One training seed and three fresh operators per study cannot establish statistical significance, a general learning advantage, production acceleration or formal security. The artifact provides a traceable basis for examining both successful generated programs and limitations, rather than a broad model or hardware ranking.

What the report covers

  • A traceable contract-to-execution pipeline for generated Triton candidates.
  • Verified fixed baselines, numerical stress tests, and clearly scoped GPU timing.
  • Real optimizer and checkpoint evidence, paired held-out comparisons, and failure analysis.
  • Limits of small evaluation sets, static source policies, and bounded compute resources.
